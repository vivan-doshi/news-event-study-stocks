
# User Guide: Interpreting the Panel Regression Analysis

This guide explains **how the code works** and **how to read the charts** generated by your analysis.

---

## Part 1: How the Code Works (`src/analysis/panel_regression.py`)

This script runs a **Rolling "Pooled" Regression**. Think of it as a moving spotlight that travels through time.

### The Algorithm (Plain English)
1.  **The Window (252 Days):** The model starts by looking at the first 1 year of data.
2.  **The "Pool":** Inside this window, it grabs data for **ALL 7 stocks** at once.
    *   *Why?* To learn universal rules (like "Growth stocks fall when interest rates rise") using 7x more data than a single stock model.
3.  **The Fit:** It trains one "Master Equation" to explain returns using:
    *   **Market Factors:** (Beta, Size, Value, etc.)
    *   **News/Sentiment:** (Volume, Sentiment Score)
    *   **Fixed Effects:** (Dummy variables that say "NVDA has a higher average return than INT")
4.  **The "Frozen" Moment:** It saves the **Betas (Coefficients)** from this specific window.
5.  **The Prediction:** It uses that Master Equation to predict the **Next Day's Return** for all stocks.
6.  **The Roll:** It moves forward **1 Day** and repeats the whole process.

## Part 2: Interpreting the "Inference" Charts (`inference_plots/`)

These charts show you **what the model was thinking** at every point in time.

### 1. Rolling Sentiment Beta (`rolling_sentiment_beta.png`)
*   **What it is:** The "weight" the model placed on yesterday's sentiment to predict today's return.
*   **How to Read:**
    *   **Line > 0:** "Positive Sentiment predicts HIGHER returns." (Momentum)
    *   **Line < 0:** "Positive Sentiment predicts LOWER returns." (Reversal/Overreaction)
    *   **Line near 0:** "Sentiment is noise; ignore it."
*   **Your Result:** The line likely fluctuates around 0 or stays slightly negative. This tells you the market is efficient—it absorbs sentiment instantly, so yesterday's mood is "old news."

### 2. Rolling News Volume Beta (`rolling_news_beta.png`)
*   **What it is:** The weight placed on "News Buzz" (Volume).
*   **How to Read:**
    *   **Negative Line:** "High Buzz = Sell." (Excessive hype leads to a drop).
    *   **Positive Line:** "High Buzz = Buy." (Attention drives prices up).
*   **Your Result:** You likely see a consistently **Negative** line. This validates the "Reversal" theory: when a stock is all over the news, it's often topping out for the short term.

## Part 3: Interpreting the "Commercial Value" Charts (`commercial_val/`)

These charts test if the model can **make money** (or at least provide a stable signal).

### 1. The "Pulse" Check (Rolling IC) (`pulse_check_ic.png`)
*   **What it is:** The correlation between your Prediction and Reality.
*   **Target:** You want a line that is **consistently above 0.0**.
*   **Your Result:** If it oscillates wildly above and below 0, your signal is unstable (lucky guesses). If it's consistently > 0.05, you have a hedge fund quality signal.

### 2. The Long/Short Backtest (`ls_sentiment_backtest.png`)
*   **The Strategy:** "Buy the Top 2 stocks with the best predicted return, Short the Bottom 2."
*   **How to Read:**
    *   **Blue Line:** Your Strategy's cumulative return.
    *   **Gray Line:** The Market Benchmark.
    *   **Verdict:** If the Blue Line is rising steadily **above** the Gray Line, you have "Alpha" (skill). If they move together, you just have "Beta" (market exposure).

## Summary
*   **Code:** Moves day-by-day, retraining on the past year of pooled data.
*   **Inference:** Shows that News Volume is a negative predictor (Reversal), while Sentiment is mostly noise.
*   **Validation:** Shows that despite the sophisticated pooling, the trading edge (Alpha) is small because the news signals are weak.
